On Eaton, CyberPower, APC, Powercom, EcoFlow, OpenUPS, Legrand and Liebert/Phoenixtec (Powerwalker, Salicru) devices every value, the status and the commands now come from the same tables that the NUT usbhid-ups driver uses (mge-hid, cps-hid, apc-hid, powercom-hid, ecoflow-hid, openups-hid, legrand-hid and liebert-hid), so they match what NUT on a PC would show. UPSes of other brands get their values and commands from one generic table built on the same NUT rows. On every UPS, `ups.status`, `ups.alarm` and `input.transfer.reason` now report the alarms and the bypass, trim/boost and off states the way usbhid-ups does.

## ⚠️ Upgrade notes

- **`ups.status` and `ups.alarm` as in NUT (all brands).** Check your Home Assistant automations and the scripts that compare `ups.status`:
  - `ups.alarm` is new. It lists the active alarms with the NUT texts (`Replace battery!`, `Shutdown imminent!`, `No battery installed!`, ...), separated by a space, and does not exist while no alarm is active. Eaton UPSes report the whole NUT list (`Fan failure!`, `Temperature too high!`, `Internal UPS fault!`, `Battery charger fail!`, ...). On Powercom UPSes the communication lost flag raises `Internal UPS fault!` and the UPS fault flag `Fan failure!`, as in NUT; on EcoFlow the communication lost flag and on OpenUPS and Liebert/Phoenixtec the internal failure flag raise `Internal UPS fault!`. On UPSes of other brands, handled by the generic driver, the communication lost flag raises `Internal UPS fault!` too;
  - with an alarm active, `ups.status` starts with `ALARM`, for example `ALARM OL RB` on a UPS that asks for a battery replacement (before: `OL RB`). upsmon sends its ALARM notification, and Home Assistant shows the alarm state;
  - new `ups.status` tokens, on the UPSes that report them: `TRIM` and `BOOST` (voltage correction, for example CyberPower `BOOST`, Liebert/Vertiv and PowerWalker `TRIM` and `BOOST`), `BYPASS` and `OFF` (Eaton, Liebert/PowerWalker: UPS output off). `CAL` (calibration) is supported too, but no model reports it yet;
  - the tokens follow the NUT order: `CHRG` before `LB`, `OVER` before `RB`. `OL LB CHRG` becomes `OL CHRG LB`. This matters only to scripts that compare the whole string: upsmon and Home Assistant read the tokens one by one;
  - `input.transfer.reason` is new: `input voltage out of range` or `input frequency out of range`, on the UPSes that report why they went on battery (Eaton). It does not exist otherwise.

  With the UPS on mains and no fault, `ups.status` is the same as v1.11.0 and the two new variables do not exist.
- **Eaton: variables now named and computed as in NUT.** Check your Home Assistant sensors and scripts:
  - `outlet.1.switch` and `outlet.2.switch` are replaced by `outlet.N.status` (`on`/`off`), one per outlet group: the old keys repeated the same value for both;
  - `input.voltage.nominal` and `input.frequency.nominal` are published only by models that report the input nominal values (NUT reads them from a different place than the output ones). On an Eaton 3S they disappear: the old values were the output ones, still available as `output.voltage.nominal` and `output.frequency.nominal`;
  - `ups.realpower`, on models that do not measure it, is estimated as NUT does, with a power factor of 0.8 (or `output.powerfactor`) instead of 0.6: on an Eaton 3S at 5 % load it goes from 21 W to 28 W;
  - new variables, as published by usbhid-ups: `ups.type` (the topology, which the firmware meant to publish but never found), `ups.timer.start`, `ups.firmware`, `device.usb.version`, `outlet.id`, `outlet.switchable`, `outlet.1.id`, `outlet.1.switchable`, `outlet.1.protect.status`, and on larger models `ups.efficiency`, `output.current`, `output.powerfactor` and the bypass values;
  - Eaton alarms of their own appear in `ups.alarm` as in NUT: `Emergency stop!`, `Wiring fault!`, `Fatal EEPROM fault!`, `Inverter AC voltage too high!`, `Inverter AC voltage too low!`, `Output short circuit!`.

  Commands and writable settings of the Eaton 3S are the same as v1.11.0.
- **Eaton: `shutdown.return` and `shutdown.stayoff` set `ups.start.auto`** (to `yes` and `no`) before the shutdown, as NUT does, on models that have this setting. It is a setting stored in the UPS.
- **Eaton 5P, 9E and similar series:**
  - `battery.capacity` is in Ah with two decimals, as in NUT;
  - `battery.capacity.full` is no longer published (it is not a standard NUT name);
  - `battery.voltage` and `battery.voltage.nominal` are published only for the series NUT reads them for;
  - with Advanced Battery Monitoring enabled, `battery.charger.status` and `battery.charger.type` appear, and `ups.status` shows `ALARM ... RB` with `ups.alarm` `No battery installed!` when the UPS reports a missing battery.

  Checked on the recorded data of an Eaton 5P, not on the hardware: if your Eaton behaves differently after the update, please open an issue and attach the USB diagnostics export.
- **CyberPower: values from the NUT table.** On the recorded CyberPower models (BR700ELCD, CP1350C, CP1500EPFCLCD, CP1600EPFCLCD, UT850EG) every variable, `ups.status` and the command list are the same as v1.11.0. A CyberPower can differ only if it reports data that NUT does not read on CyberPower:
  - `ups.status` no longer uses the shutdown imminent, battery present, fully discharged and communication lost flags, nor the general overload flag (the overload still comes from the output, as in NUT);
  - the status flags are read only from the `PresentStatus` collection, as in NUT: a CyberPower that reports mains, charging or low battery only outside it would show `Unknown`;
  - `battery.capacity.full` is no longer published (it is not a standard NUT name);
  - load, voltages, frequencies, powers and the test result are read only where NUT reads them on CyberPower;
  - `ups.temperature` is converted as in NUT: from kelvin when the value is between 273 and 373, otherwise as it is (before, every value above 200 was taken as kelvin);
  - `shutdown.reboot` is offered only on models that have the output reboot timer;
  - `battery.type`, `ups.firmware` and `battery.mfr.date`, when they come from a USB string, are cut to 19 characters as in NUT;
  - `battery.capacity`, on the models that report the battery design capacity, has two decimals as in NUT (`10` becomes `10.00`).

  Checked on recorded data, not on the hardware: if your CyberPower behaves differently after the update, please open an issue and attach the USB diagnostics export.
- **APC: values from the NUT table.** `ups.status` is the same as v1.11.0 on the thirteen recordings of nine APC models (Back-UPS BGM2200, BX750MI, BX1500G, CS 500, CS 750-RS, ES 700G, RS 900MI, XS 700U, Smart-UPS 750). Some values change, as in NUT:
  - `battery.voltage` has one decimal (`13.60` becomes `13.6`);
  - on the Smart-UPS (and the Back-UPS CS 500), `ups.load` has one decimal and is no longer truncated (`40` becomes `40.3`), and `output.voltage.nominal` is `230.0`;
  - new variables where the UPS reports them: `ups.timer.reboot`, `ups.timer.start` and `ups.timer.shutdown` (also from the APC timers the firmware could not read before), `battery.charge.warning`, `output.current`, and `ambient.temperature` / `ambient.humidity` with an APC environmental probe;
  - `input.transfer.reason` shows why the UPS last went on battery (`input voltage out of range`, `input frequency out of range`), from the APC line fail cause, as in NUT. It can stay set after the UPS is back on mains;
  - `ups.status` shows `OFF` on the models that report the APC status flag 0 (output off), as in NUT;
  - the battery replacement date of the PowerSummary collection is `battery.mfr.date` (NUT name) instead of `battery.date`, and a date the UPS reports as 0 is `not set`;
  - the status flags NUT does not read on APC (fully charged, fully discharged, communication lost) are no longer used, and `battery.capacity.full` is no longer published;
  - `battery.capacity`, on the models that report the battery design capacity, has two decimals as in NUT (`10` becomes `10.00`);
  - on models with the APC status flag that is not already read with the status (for example the Back-UPS ES 700G), its report is polled every 2 s with the status, as in NUT. The Back-UPS BX polls are unchanged.

  The commands are the same as v1.11.0. Checked on recorded data, not on the hardware: if your APC behaves differently after the update, please open an issue and attach the USB diagnostics export.

- **Powercom: values from the NUT table.** Checked on the recorded descriptor of the SPD-750U (issue #21) and on simulated data, not on the hardware. On the SPD-750U:
  - new variables, which the firmware did not read before: `battery.charge.low`, `battery.charge.warning`, `battery.mfr.date`, `battery.type`, `ups.date` (the UPS manufacturing date, NUT name for Powercom), `ups.test.result`, `ups.timer.start`, `ups.timer.shutdown`, `input.voltage.nominal`, `input.frequency`, `output.voltage.nominal`, `output.frequency`;
  - `battery.voltage` is the text the UPS sends, as in NUT (`13.70` becomes `13.7`), and `0.0` until the first valid reading;
  - the command list has only `test.battery.start.quick`, as in NUT: `test.battery.start.deep` and `test.battery.stop` are gone. There are still no load or shutdown commands on Powercom;
  - the poll reads all the values NUT reads: 22 requests in the first 30 s, then about 15 every 30 s instead of 7, still 800 ms apart (issue #36). The values that never change (dates, battery type, nominal voltages) are read once. The status is read every 2 s from both status reports of the UPS, and right after the UPS reports a change, so a power failure shows within about a second. If values drop to 0 or the System Log shows USB errors after the update, please open an issue and attach the USB diagnostics export.

  On the other Powercom models (IMPERIAL, Smart King Pro, WOW, Vanguard, Black Knight Pro) the vendor status byte gives `TRIM`, `BOOST`, `OVER`, `LB`, `RB`, `CAL` (battery test running) and `Fan failure!` (UPS fault, the NUT text) as in NUT, and `beeper.mute` is no longer offered (NUT has no such command for Powercom). `beeper.disable` (and `beeper.toggle` when it disables) writes 0 as in NUT, instead of 2. `battery.capacity` is no longer published (NUT does not read the Powercom design capacity), and a UPS fault raises `Fan failure!` (the NUT text) instead of an internal flag with no effect. A Powercom that reports the beeper as a single bit shows no `ups.beeper.status` while the beeper is off, as in NUT.
- **EcoFlow: values from the NUT table.** Checked on simulated data of the River 3 Plus and Delta 3 Plus layout, not on the hardware: please report any difference (PR #66).
  - `battery.runtime.low` is in minutes, as NUT shows it for EcoFlow (`120` on a Delta 3 Plus, `600` on a River 3 Plus; before: `7200` and `36000`, in seconds). `battery.runtime` stays in seconds;
  - new variables, as in NUT: `ups.beeper.status`, and four `experimental.ups.powersummary.*` variables (OEM information, shutdown requested, voltage not regulated, rechargeable). `battery.voltage` has one decimal (`51.0`, before `51.00`);
  - `ups.timer.shutdown` and `ups.timer.reboot` come only from the outlet timers, as in NUT;
  - a negative run time no longer removes `battery.runtime`: the last value stays, as in NUT;
  - no instant commands: NUT leaves the EcoFlow beeper and shutdown commands out, since the devices ignore them. The battery test commands that a device with a battery test field could get before are gone too;
  - `ups.mfr` is `EcoFlow` when the device has no manufacturer string.
- **OpenUPS (WalleCube W150 and similar): values from the NUT table.** Checked on the recording of issue #26:
  - the voltages have two decimals and the currents three, as in NUT (`output.voltage` `12.24`, before `12.2`; `output.current` `1.340`, before `1.34`); `battery.temperature` has two decimals between the points of the thermistor table (`29.47`, before `29.5`);
  - `ups.temperature` is gone: it was the raw thermistor reading (`5.5` on the W150), not a temperature. The battery temperature is `battery.temperature`;
  - new variables: `battery.capacity`, `battery.charge.warning`, and `battery.type` from the device strings (`LiFePO4` on the W150);
  - the 0xD004 models use the NUT voltage and current scales (before, the 0xD005 ones);
  - `ups.status` is unchanged: the battery present flag is still ignored, because some units report it wrong (issue #26), so no `RB` and no `No battery installed!`. `ups.load` and `ups.realpower` are still estimated from the output voltage and current: NUT does not publish them for OpenUPS.
- **Legrand (Keor PDU, Keor SP): values from the NUT table.** Checked on the recording of the Keor PDU 800 (issue #90), not on the hardware:
  - `input.voltage` and `output.voltage` have no decimals and `output.frequency` is a whole number, as in NUT (`230` and `50`, before `230.2` and `50.0`);
  - `battery.voltage` and `battery.voltage.nominal` have one decimal (`13.7` and `12.0`, before `13.70` and `12`). The values stay the right ones: NUT's Legrand conversions, which give values off by a factor of 10 on this model, are still not applied;
  - the command list no longer has `shutdown.stop`, which NUT does not offer on Legrand. The other commands are unchanged, `beeper.enable` still writes 2 and `beeper.disable` 3.
- **Liebert / Phoenixtec (Powerwalker, Salicru): values from the NUT table.** Checked on the recordings of the Powerwalker VI 2200 SHL (issue #96), not on the hardware. The values and the command list are unchanged. `ups.status` now shows `OFF` when the UPS reports its output as not good (`OL CHRG OFF` on the first recording of #96), and the internal failure flag raises `ALARM` with `Internal UPS fault!`, as in NUT.

- **UPSes of other brands (generic driver): values from a generic table.** Checked by replaying the recordings of all the brands above as if they came from an unknown brand. The variables and the command list are the same as v1.11.0, with these differences:
  - `battery.voltage` has one decimal (`13.60` becomes `13.6`), and the values that were truncated are rounded, as in NUT;
  - the communication lost flag raises `ALARM` with `Internal UPS fault!`, as in NUT (before, an internal flag with no effect);
  - `battery.capacity` has two decimals (`10.00`), `battery.charge` is no longer cut at 100, and `battery.capacity.full` is no longer published (it is not a standard NUT name);
  - a battery or UPS manufacturing date the UPS reports as 0 is `not set`, as in NUT, instead of missing.

  If your UPS of another brand behaves differently after the update, please open an issue and attach the USB diagnostics export.

- **Settings changed on the UPS panel show after the next command (all brands).** Nominal values, transfer limits, battery thresholds and the other settings NUT marks as static or semi-static are read when the UPS connects and again after every setting change (`upsrw`, web UI) or command (`upscmd`), no longer every 30 s, as usbhid-ups does. A setting changed from the UPS front panel or the manufacturer's software shows after the next setting change, command or reconnection.

## Other changes

- **Eaton driver:** values, status flags and instant commands are rows of the mge-hid table, copied from NUT line by line, instead of hand-written mappings. The firmware writes a value only when it changes, which saves memory allocations at every USB report.
- **CyberPower driver:** the same for the cps-hid table. The CyberPower-specific fixes stay: the battery voltage and frequency scales of the 0x0501 models, the 60/120 s delays, the reports that some firmwares must never be asked for, and the `ups.realpower` estimate on models that do not measure it.
- **APC driver:** the same for the apc-hid table. The APC-specific fixes stay: the model and firmware split of the product string (issue #76), and the slower poll and longer requests of the Back-UPS BX (issue #60). The Back-UPS BGM2200 readings of issue #98 (fields read from their first occurrence, `OL DISCHRG`) are unchanged.
- **Powercom driver:** the same for the powercom-hid table. The Powercom-specific choices stay: 800 ms between USB requests and the status keep-alive of the SPD-750U (issue #36), and the battery voltage read from the legacy report 0xA4. `ups.mfr`, `ups.model` and `ups.serial` are the USB strings as in NUT: a Powercom without them shows `ups.mfr` `PowerCOM` and no `ups.model` / `ups.serial` (before, a series name chosen from the USB product ID).
- **Legrand and Liebert drivers:** the same for the legrand-hid and liebert-hid tables. What stays outside the tables: the Legrand beeper encoding (2 enabled, 3 disabled) and its `ups.beeper.status`. The usage names of the NUT tables are compared ignoring case, as NUT does, and a `beeper.toggle` row of its own (Liebert) is enough to offer the toggle.
- **Generic driver:** the last hand-written mappings are gone. Every value and command of every driver comes from a NUT table row; for the other brands from a generic table with the paths and formats of the NUT tables. The firmware is about 16 KB (ESP32-S3) to 19 KB (ESP32-P4) smaller.
- **Fewer USB requests (all brands):** the full poll every 30 s no longer reads the reports that hold only static or semi-static values, as usbhid-ups. Over the 24 UPS recordings of the test suite the full poll goes from 827 to 773 requests; on an Eaton 3S from 24 to 14 or 16, up to 20 requests a minute fewer. After a setting change or a command the next poll reads them again right away.
- **EcoFlow and OpenUPS drivers:** the same for the ecoflow-hid and openups-hid tables. What stays outside the tables: no beeper or shutdown commands on EcoFlow, the voltage scales of the OpenUPS product IDs, the OpenUPS battery present flag ignored and the `ups.load` estimate.
